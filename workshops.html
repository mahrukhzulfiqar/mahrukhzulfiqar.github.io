Workshop for product, design and engineering teams

The Autonomy Ladder: how much freedom should your AI agent get?

Agents are moving from demos into production. The hard question is no longer what an agent can do, but what it should be allowed to do on its own. The goal isn't maximum autonomy. It's the right autonomy for the risk.

Book the workshopHow it works

Why teams book it

Most teams give an agent its freedom one feature at a time, and nobody decides it on purpose. Product wants speed, engineering wants control, legal wants to know who is accountable. The workshop gets those people in one room and gives them one shared way to decide.

A shared decision

For each action your agent can take, the team agrees how much freedom it gets today, and why.

A guardrail list

What has to be true before the agent earns more autonomy: checks, approvals, tests and limits to build next.

A common language

Product, design, engineering and risk people leave with the same words for autonomy, so the next decision takes minutes, not meetings.

How it works

  1. Bring an agent. One your team is building or planning, or one of the example agents I bring.
  2. Work it through, hands-on. No laptops. Small groups build, challenge and pressure-test the agent's design against four questions:
    • What's the cost of this agent being wrong?
    • Is the action reversible?
    • Where does a human need to stay in the loop?
    • Can your guardrails actually hold?
  3. Decide together. Groups compare where they landed and why, and the team leaves with a decision and next steps.
